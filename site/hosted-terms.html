What we run for you

We rent a virtual private server on your behalf, install isomux on it, and serve it at the name you picked, over HTTPS. Setup usually finishes in under ten minutes, and you get a sign-in link when it is ready.

The price covers the server. The agents run on your own Claude or ChatGPT plan, which you sign in to from inside the office. We do not resell model usage and never sit between you and your model provider.

Exporting your work is also yours to do. See cancellation below for when the window closes.

Your content

Everything you and your agents create on the server is yours. We take no license to it, and the access rules below mean we cannot read it.

Getting into your own server

You can paste your own SSH key at signup, and we strongly recommend it. It gives you root administrator access to the VPS, which lets you install system software, manage the server, recover access to your Isomux office if your browser no longer has access, and verify that our SSH key is gone. Isomux agents and the built-in terminal cannot use sudo; this protects the VPS from accidental or unsafe agent commands.

If you skip it, you cannot add administrator access later from inside Isomux. Once setup is done, you cannot install system software, manage the VPS, or recover office access yourself if your browser loses access. You must ask us in writing to use the provider's rescue console under the access rules below. If you would rather not authorize that, or the office is not running, the route left is a reinstall, which erases the disk. You can ask a chatbot to help you create an SSH key and find the public key to paste at signup.

Our access to your server

We hold an SSH key to your server while we set it up, and only then. It is removed when you confirm from your office that you are in, or automatically after 30 days, whichever comes first. We confirm the removal by trying to reconnect with the key we just deleted and requiring that attempt to fail. Our copy is then destroyed. After that we have no way into your running office, and we never see what is inside it.

If you later ask us in writing to look at a specific problem, we can open the provider's rescue console, which reaches the disk. We do that only on that request, we record what we do, and the access ends with the problem.

What we keep as the renter

The provider account is ours, so provider-level controls stay ours: reboot, power off, power on, reinstall, and the rescue console, which follows the rules in the section above. The power controls we use in three situations, and no others: when you ask (your account dashboard's reboot button is one of them), when a payment fails, and when your subscription ends. Both of the latter are described below.

This is administrative control, not a way to read your files, and it is not something we can technically give up while we hold the account.

For comparison: the rescue capability is not something we added - it comes with renting servers at all. Every major provider documents a rescue environment available to whoever holds the account (Contabo, Hetzner, DigitalOcean), which is why we spell out how we govern it instead of pretending it does not exist. Managed hosts of similar products keep wider access than ours: OneClickClaw, for example, offers customers opt-in SSH for its own staff and logs those sessions, commands included (terms, privacy policy).

If a payment fails

Stripe retries on its own schedule. Until the retries run out, the office keeps running as normal, so you can take your work out at any time. Then we suspend it by powering the server off; your data stays on the disk, untouched, and the office comes back exactly as it was when the payment goes through. After a month suspended, the server is released and everything on it is gone. Cancelling partway through a period does not refund the rest of it. Two exceptions: your first week - cancel within 7 days of first signing up and we refund in full - and cases where we failed to deliver, at our discretion.

When a new isomux release is out, your office tells you and you apply it when you choose, so updates happen on your schedule, not ours; a failed update rolls back on its own.
