# Ask replies

Part of the `asks` page: when the member dismisses, postpones or automates your ask, asks a question about it, or asks you to rewrite it.

If the member dismisses it, the bracket says `Dismissed your ask` and the message is `Dismissed` or the reason the member typed.

If the member postpones it, the bracket says `Postponed your ask <id>, now closed` and the message is `Not now` or the member's note. When the note names a time (`Circle back at: Monday 9am`), schedule a message to yourself for then (`scheduled-messages` page) and open the ask again then if it still applies. For an app's ask, open your own ask then and name the app's ask in it. When the note asks you to file the ask as a task, file it on the board (`tasks` page). Otherwise, without a time, decide when to ask again, or go on without an answer.

A member message that starts with `About your ask "<the start of your question>":` (or the same in the member's language) is a question about that open ask, not its answer. Answer it in the chat and keep the ask open; edit the ask if your answer changes it.

When the member asks for a rewrite, you get `[Rewrite your ask <id> in place, ...: "<prompt>"]` (`app <name>'s ask` for an app's ask) and what they want changed: shorter, more context, visuals (`readFiles` with an image or a diagram), or a question to answer inside the ask. With no words of their own, the message asks for plain words: what the member must decide or do and your pick, in at most two sentences, without internal names or ids. Edit the ask in place with `PATCH`, not a new ask and not a chat answer. Until your next change of the ask it has `simplifyRequestedAt`; after a change of the prompt, `originalPrompt` holds the first prompt. If the member withdraws the request first, you get `[Simplify request withdrawn for your ask <id>: "<prompt>"]`: leave the ask as it is.

When the member answers with "... and automate", a second bracket follows the first: `[Automate asks like this: "<their words>". ...]`, or `[Automate asks like this; propose the rule. ...]` when they left the rule to you. The ask stays open: it has `automateRequest` `{at, words, answer}`, with the answer you got. First act on the answer. Then add the rule to the same ask, not a new one: `PATCH` it with only `step` `{"kind": "instructions-line", "scope": "agent", "line": "<one line>"}`. The line says which asks you decide alone, how, and what you still ask about. `scope` is `agent` (your instructions), `room` (your room's) or `member` (your manager's, for all their agents): the narrowest that reaches the agents that get such asks. Apply adds the line at the end, as sent, and closes the ask. The edit refuses 400 for more than one line, over 500 characters or characters that do not show, 409 `step_done` when the instructions hold the line, 403 `member_not_allowed` when the member cannot change them, and 409 `answered` for any field but `step`. If you cannot write a rule, delete the ask: it closes as answered. To propose a rule with no such answer, open an action ask (`action-asks` page) with this step.

With a rewrite request, the bracket comes too, and `automateRequest` has no `answer`: rewrite the ask, and add the rule after the member answers it (before, the `step` is refused 409 `not_answered`). That answer brings the bracket again. A withdrawn rewrite request takes back the automate request too.

On the ask with the rule, the member can ask for a change: `[Change the rule proposed on your ask <id> in place: "<line>"]` and what to change. `PATCH` a new `instructions-line` step. If they do not want the rule, you get `[Rule not wanted for your ask <id>, now closed: ...]`: do not propose it again.

To suggest the rule on an ask, send `automateText` when you open or edit it. Not on a step ask or an app's ask (422 `invalid_automate`).
